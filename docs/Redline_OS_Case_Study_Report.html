1. Abstract

Hospital computing environments must simultaneously orchestrate time-critical emergency care (cardiac arrest, multi-organ trauma, stroke), continuous ICU ventilator telemetry, stat diagnostic lab panels, outpatient queues, pharmacy dispatches, and inter-hospital patient transfers. When an origin facility reaches capacity, manual phone coordination wastes golden minutes, exposes stale bed counts, and risks double-booking the last available ICU bed.

Redline (HEMS-Link) models a ten-hospital emergency coordination network centred on Jain University Health Centre (Kanakapura Road, South Bengaluru) as a deterministic Operating System kernel. Every clinical activity executes as a five-state Process Control Block (PCB); ICU beds and ventilators act as counting semaphores; concurrent reservations run inside a mutex-protected critical section with two-phase holds and optimistic version tokens; and hospital responses flow via asynchronous IPC queues.

Eight scheduling policies — FCFS, SJF, SRTF, Non-Preemptive Priority, Preemptive Priority, Round Robin (Q=3), Real-Time EDF, and MLFQ — were evaluated on the eight-process baseline (T1–T8) and a ten-process hospital clinical mix (H1–H10). While SJF achieves the lowest overall wait (11.0 ticks), Preemptive Priority delivers 0.00-tick critical response and is paired with priority aging (−1 every 10 ticks) to prevent starvation. In a 1,000-trial concurrency test, mutex protection reduces double-bookings from 692/1,000 to 0/1,000.

2. Introduction & Problem Statement

A modern hospital computer system is an archetypal concurrent, resource-constrained environment. Emergency admissions arrive unpredictably — like hardware interrupts — while routine outpatient registrations, laboratory batches, pharmacy refills, and planned step-down transfers compete for the same CPU scheduler and clinical staff attention. Extending coordination across multiple hospitals introduces distributed-systems hazards: availability figures age quickly, two referring hospitals can view the same free ventilator simultaneously, and a patient holding an ICU bed while waiting for a ventilator can trigger a circular wait.

Problem Statement. Design and analyse an Operating Systems-driven hospital emergency management and coordination architecture that efficiently schedules emergency patients, ICU monitoring, laboratory tests, OP queues, pharmacy requests, and inter-hospital transfers — ensuring hard real-time responsiveness for critical emergencies without starvation, race conditions, stale-data dispatch, or multi-resource deadlocks.

3. Objectives, Scope & Explicit Assumptions

3.1 Case Study Objectives

This study was undertaken with the following concrete goals:

  • Model diverse hospital activities as OS processes with defined arrival times, priorities, burst times, and SLA deadlines.
  • Apply and compare FCFS, SJF, Preemptive Priority, and Round Robin (Q=3), alongside SRTF, Non-Preemptive Priority, Real-Time EDF, and MLFQ.
  • Compute Completion Time, Turnaround Time, Waiting Time, Response Time, CPU Utilisation, Throughput, and SLA Compliance.
  • Classify tasks into Hard Real-Time, Soft Real-Time, and Non-Real-Time categories and examine the impact of continuously arriving emergencies.
  • Prevent race conditions, starvation, priority inversion, and deadlocks using Mutex locks, Counting Semaphores, Two-Phase Holds, Aging, Resource Ordering, RAG detection, and Banker's Algorithm.

3.2 Engineering Assumptions & Scope

To ensure reproducible comparisons across all eight scheduling algorithms, the following engineering assumptions were established at the outset:

  • Normalised Time Quantum. CPU burst times and arrivals are expressed in normalised scheduling ticks (1 tick = 1 unit of dispatch/processing work) for reproducible cross-algorithm mathematics.
  • Single-CPU Scheduler Core. The core scheduling comparison evaluates single-processor preemption and context switching; multi-core SMP extension is documented in Section 12.
  • Simulated Availability & Telemetry. Hospital bed counts, trust scores, and coordinates represent realistic Kanakapura–Bengaluru locations with synthetic capacities.
  • Decision-Support Boundary. Automated matching and two-phase holds reserve capacity tentatively; final clinical admission always requires receiving hospital acceptance.

4. System Description — 10-Hospital Network

Redline connects the origin node — Jain University Health Centre (Kanakapura Rd) — to nine receiving hospitals across South Bengaluru and Kanakapura. On the Live Dispatch console, staff submit patient or internal hospital tasks; the OS Engine admits a PCB, ranks eligible hospitals using Haversine ETA (50%), clinical trust (20%), data freshness (15%), and incoming load (15%), and executes a mutex-protected two-phase hold.

8. Impact of Continuously Arriving Emergencies

Under strict static Preemptive Priority, a sustained burst of Priority-1 emergencies creates two well-known real-time hazards: indefinite starvation of routine tasks (such as OP discharges or planned dialysis transfers) and priority inversion when a low-priority task holds a shared availability lock.

PhenomenonExperimental SetupWithout MitigationWith Redline Mitigation
Starvation under Continuous P1 Stream P1 arrives every 4 ticks for 120 ticks; P8 arrives at t=1 (BT=2). P8 first dispatch: Starved (>120 ticks) Aging (−1 every 10 ticks) boosts P8 → P1; dispatched at t=72.
Priority Inversion on Availability Lock P8 holds lock; P1 needs lock at t=2; P4 medium task arrives. P4 preempts P8; P1 delayed 12 ticks (until t=14). Priority Inheritance boosts P8 → P1; P1 unblocked in 2 ticks (t=4).

9. Synchronisation, Two-Phase Holds & Deadlock Handling

Each hospital facility acts as a Counting Semaphore (S = free_units). Checking and decrementing S executes inside a Mutex Critical Section coupled with an optimistic version token. Rather than blocking the CPU while waiting for a hospital reply, Redline uses a Two-Phase Hold (PREPARE → COMMITTED / ABORTED / EXPIRED) and supports Top-N Parallel Referral where the first hospital to accept commits its hold and atomically releases all sibling holds.

Concurrency Race Proof (1,000 Trials). Without mutex protection, 692 of 1,000 concurrent last-bed reservation attempts result in double-bookings. With the threading.Lock mutex and compare-and-swap version token in place, this figure drops to 0 of 1,000 — a complete elimination of the race condition under test conditions.

9.1 Deadlock Prevention, RAG Detection & Banker's Safe-State Check

When a critical patient requires both an ICU Bed (R1) and a Ventilator (R2), unconstrained acquisition can satisfy all four Coffman conditions (Mutual Exclusion, Hold-and-Wait, No Preemption, Circular Wait). Redline layers three defences:

  • Global Resource Ordering (ICU → Emergency → Ventilator → Neuro → Specialist) — breaks the Circular Wait condition outright.
  • Resource Allocation Graph (RAG) Cycle Detection — releases the lowest-priority victim when a cycle is detected.
  • Banker's Algorithm Safe-State Verification — demonstrated on Hospital B: Total=[3,2,1], Available=[1,1,0].

10. Proposed Hybrid Policy & Expected OS Behaviour

Redline adopts a hybrid Preemptive Priority + Aging (−1 per 10 ticks) kernel paired with EDF Real-Time SLA Deadline Monitoring during normal operations, and switches Priority-1 emergencies to Surge FIFO + Capacity Load Balancing (suspending P1 aging and skipping stale nodes) during mass-casualty surges.

Expected OS Behaviour QuestionVerified Kernel Response in Redline
1. Which process should execute? The highest-priority READY process (or earliest-deadline task under EDF) immediately preempts any lower-priority running task.
2. Which process should wait? Preempted routine tasks return to READY; referrals awaiting hospital confirmation enter WAITING without blocking the CPU.
3. How are resources allocated? The matcher filters free semaphores, ranks hospitals by ETA/Trust/Freshness/Load, and locks resources in fixed global order.
4. How is synchronisation maintained? Mutex guards wait(S)/signal(S) critical sections; optimistic version tokens reject stale edits with HTTP 409.
5. How does it recover from failure? TTL expiry workers abort timed-out holds, restore S, and escalate; the RAG cycle detector preempts the lowest-priority victim.

12. Recommended Additional Features

  • Multi-Core SMP Hospital CPU Scheduling. Extend the single-CPU scheduler to N-core symmetric multiprocessing with dedicated core affinity for Emergency Triage, ICU Telemetry, and Lab Processing.
  • Rate Monotonic Scheduling (RMS) for Periodic ICU Vitals. Model continuous ECG/SpO2/ventilator polling as periodic tasks (Ci, Ti) with Liu–Layland utilisation bound checks.
  • Persistent SQLite (WAL Mode) + Server-Sent Events (SSE). Replace in-memory state with concurrent WAL-mode SQLite persistence and real-time SSE push updates across dispatch terminals.
  • Road-Network Dijkstra/A* Ambulance Routing. Upgrade Haversine ETA to graph-based shortest-path routing weighted by live Bengaluru traffic multipliers.
  • Real-Time EDF Admission Control & HL7/FHIR Stubs. Shed or defer non-critical OP tasks when CPU utilisation threatens hard real-time deadlines, and expose FHIR ServiceRequest payloads.

13. Conclusion

Redline (HEMS-Link) demonstrates that hospital emergency management and multi-hospital coordination map directly onto core Operating Systems abstractions. Evaluating eight scheduling policies confirms that overall average waiting time alone is clinically deceptive: SJF minimises aggregate wait (11.00 ticks) while delaying a cardiac arrest until t=25, whereas Preemptive Priority delivers 0.00-tick critical response.

When combined with Aging (−1 per 10 ticks), EDF SLA monitoring, mutex-protected two-phase holds, Banker's algorithm checks, and RAG deadlock recovery, Redline guarantees responsiveness, fairness, and deadlock safety across all evaluated scenarios — while leaving final clinical judgement where it belongs: with the receiving hospital staff.
